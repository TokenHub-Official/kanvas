# Product

## Purpose

Kanvas connects briefs, source material, AI or browser-local processing, canvas
editing, variant comparison, human review, delivery, and asset reuse for
professional visual teams. Success means shorter production time, more usable
first drafts, and greater asset reuse while keeping every accepted result
traceable to its inputs, implementation, version, duration, and cost.

## Users

- Designers and content producers create and review work in a desktop browser.
- Workspace administrators manage members, models, budgets, resource sources,
  and audit access.
- Self-hosting teams operate their own database, storage, providers, and
  security policy.
- Open-source contributors extend capabilities, workflows, provider adapters,
  and resource sources.

## Boundaries

- Kanvas does not reproduce Photoshop, Canva, or a model-provider product.
- Canvas, Agent, Workflow, Asset, and Brand are peer product modules.
- Project and Deliverable organize business work and delivery; they are not
  mandatory setup steps for every creation.
- Browser, Worker, and Provider are execution targets. Kanvas owns permissions,
  budgets, Artifacts, audits, and business state.
- Machine success is not business acceptance. A human decision pins a specific
  immutable result.
- Versioned Workflows and Skills organize concrete scenarios such as posters or
  commerce images; the platform core does not hard-code them.
- The UI uses business language and does not expose internal IDs, credentials,
  database details, or tool orchestration.

## Principles

1. Optimize for completing a deliverable, not discovering tools or models.
2. Keep the work visually central and make the process recoverable and
   explainable.
3. Preserve permission, version, lineage, cost, and audit facts for consequential
   actions.
4. Adopt external resources into internal Artifacts or pinned revisions before
   they enter durable documents.
5. Internal deployments and community self-hosting use the same core and
   extension model.
